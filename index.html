AI governance

Institutional AI readiness programme

At a SUNY community college, I led a campus-wide AI usage and attitudes survey, co-developed an AI Integration Strategic Plan, helped stand up an AI Advisory Committee, and authored AI guidance for faculty, staff and leadership. The survey is what made it work: it turned an abstract argument into evidence about what people were already doing, which is a far better place to start writing policy from. I've since discussed the approach publicly on the Higher Ed Pulse podcast.

  • Survey design
  • Strategic plan
  • Committee governance
  • Policy authoring

Process

How I work

No discovery-phase theatre, no twelve-week runway before you see anything real. Open a step to read more.

01

Start with the problem

A short conversation about what's actually costing you time or money. Sometimes the answer is that you don't need the thing you asked for — I'll tell you that.

02

Find out what's true

Ask the people involved before deciding anything. A survey, an audit, a walk through the current workflow. Assumptions are where projects go wrong.

03

Build in the open

You see working drafts early and often, on a live preview link. Course corrections happen while they're still cheap.

04

Hand over the keys

Your code, your accounts, your documents, documented. Stay for ongoing support if it's useful — but never because you're locked in.

About

Who you'd be working with

I'm Edward Joseph Tatton III — Edward is fine. For nearly twenty years I've worked in creative communications at a SUNY community college, where I manage the team responsible for branding, marketing and web communication — the print, photography, video and design work that drives enrollment and keeps an institution recognisable to itself.

Over the past year I've become deeply involved in how that institution thinks about, governs and integrates AI: running the survey, co-writing the strategic plan, helping form the advisory committee, and authoring the guidance. It has given me a practitioner's view of what it actually takes to move an organization from AI anxiety to AI readiness — which is mostly not a technology problem.

ET3 Media is the studio I run alongside that, for small business clients. I've also taught graphic design as an adjunct, and I hold a master's in Information Design & Technology and a bachelor's in Photography and Art History.
