01

Civic news · Live

The Norwalk Sound

A daily civic brief for Norwalk, Connecticut. It reads the city's meeting agendas, packets and minutes, local news, public filings, weather and tides, and publishes before breakfast — with a Sunday newsletter and a full Spanish edition.

The public record is public but scattered across portals nobody has time to read. The Sound puts it in one place. Anything interpretive is AI-drafted and held for human review; the record itself is published verbatim; no names from police logs, ever.

Read the Sound →

02

Accountability · Live

Nothing To See Here

A running, sourced timeline of how license-plate readers, body cameras, AI report-writers and the rest of the "public-safety technology" market are actually going — and what the failures cost taxpayers. THE TAB, in the corner, adds up the documented public money.

The format is borrowed, with credit, from Web3 Is Going Just Great. Nothing publishes until code has confirmed a fetched source supports the claim, the date and the dollar figure. The entries are deadpan; the facts carry the irony.

Built for a few people

Family archive · Private

FamilyBot

A private archive for two families: a tree, people pages, recipes, photos and stories, built to one standard — could your grandmother use it on her phone, alone, on the first try?

※ No link: invite-only, by design.

Personal briefing · Private

HealthIntel

A news-intelligence briefing built for one reader. It clusters the same healthcare story across outlets and writes a daily brief under one rule: never state a fact that wasn't in the headline or the blurb.

※ No link: single-reader tool behind a login.

Workplace tools · Internal

Marketing Dashboard & Ad Sandbox

Two browser-only tools for a college marketing team: campaign CSVs into charts and branded PDFs, and ad copy tested against fifteen synthetic student personas before it runs. No install, no server; the data never leaves the machine.

※ No link: lives on an internal network drive.

About

I'm Edward Joseph Tatton III. Ed is fine. I've spent nearly twenty years in creative communications at a college in New York, where I lead the team behind its brand, print, photography and web — and where I more recently led its AI readiness work: the survey, the strategic plan, the advisory committee and the guidance.

ET3 Media is the studio I run alongside that. Everything else here is what I build when nobody asked me to.

I hold a master's in Information Design & Technology and a bachelor's in Photography and Art History, and I've taught graphic design as an adjunct. I live in Norwalk, Connecticut.
