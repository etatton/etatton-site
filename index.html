How I work

No discovery-phase theatre, no twelve-week runway before you see anything real.

01 Start with the problem

A short conversation about what's actually costing you time or money. Sometimes the answer is that you don't need custom software at all — I'll tell you that.

02 Scope something small

A fixed, clearly defined first piece with a real deliverable. You know the price and the timeline before anything starts.

03 Build in the open

You see working software early and often, on a live preview link. Course corrections happen while they're still cheap.

04 Hand over the keys

Your code, your accounts, your data, documented. Stay for ongoing support if it's useful — but never because you're locked in.

About

I work with small businesses and small teams — the ones big enough to have real operational problems, but not big enough to keep an engineering department on payroll.

Most of what I build is unglamorous and specific: a portal that saves a coordinator six hours a week, a form that stops data being retyped three times, a site that loads before the visitor gives up. I care much more about whether a thing is still working smoothly a year from now than whether it used an interesting framework.

Working independently means you deal with the person doing the work. No account manager, no handoff to a junior team, no billing surprises.
